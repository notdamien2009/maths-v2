Reveal comparison model

Examples · complete worked models

Example 1 · A shared idea

Question: How does family memory shape both speakers' sense of belonging?

Model: Both speakers connect belonging to memories passed down through family. In The Lantern at the Door, the grandmother “keeps the flame alive”, suggesting that family care preserves a welcoming home. In The Road Between Hills, the father says “Remember where your stories start”, making family memory a guide for someone who is leaving. Therefore, both poems value memory, but the first imagines home as a place to return to, whereas the second presents belonging as something that can travel.

Example 2 · A technique with an effect

Question: Compare the poems' symbols.

Model: The lantern is a symbol of safety: its “small and steady flame” continues to welcome travellers despite the rain. By contrast, the father's stone is carried by the speaker and symbolises origins that remain meaningful away from home. Both physical objects suggest continuity, but they work in different directions.

Example 3 · Avoid an inaccurate cultural claim

Weak: Everyone in mountain communities carries stones to remember their family.

Improved: In this imagined speaker's story, the stone becomes a personal reminder of family origins. The text alone cannot establish a custom for a real community.
