Year 9 · Assessment 030

Parent-only marking guide — Unseen pre-1914 prose · 40 marks

Keep this file separate from the pupil assessment. Mark after submission.

Marking principle

Accept any defensible interpretation supported by precise evidence and explanation. Do not demand the exact sample phrasing. Award marks for demonstrated understanding and method analysis; do not reward quotation without explanation. Total 40; suggested pass threshold 28/40 (70%).

A1 · 2 marks

Marking: Award one mark for each valid detail, maximum 2.

Illustrative answer / look for: The narrator says “Marley was dead” and that there is “no doubt whatever”; formal witnesses signed the burial register; Scrooge signed it. Any two distinct details.

A2 · 4 marks

Marking: Two points, each worth one interpretation plus one appropriate supporting detail.

Illustrative answer / look for: Scrooge was Marley’s business partner (line 10); he handled Marley’s estate as executor/administrator (line 11); he is described as the only friend and mourner (line 11), suggesting isolation; he prioritised business at the funeral (line 12).

A3 · 6 marks

Marking: 1–2: basic meaning; 3–4: explained device and effect with quotation; 5–6: developed discussion of repetition, comparison and narrator tone.

Illustrative answer / look for: Repeated insistence that Marley is dead establishes certainty; the familiar “door-nail” simile is comic and colloquial, making the narrator sound informal, while insisting on death as a prerequisite for later supernatural events. Accept foreshadowing when explained.

B1 · 6 marks

Marking: 1–2: relevant observation; 3–4: connected comment and quotation; 5–6: thoughtful analysis of direct address, voice and humorous digression.

Illustrative answer / look for: “Mind!” directly calls for attention; “I don’t mean to say” imitates conversation; coffin-nail versus door-nail digression is deliberately absurd, creating a playful, self-aware narrator; “You will therefore permit me” addresses the audience personally.

B2 · 6 marks

Marking: 1–2: a relevant point; 3–4: supported explanation; 5–6: perceptive discussion of accumulated roles and contrast.

Illustrative answer / look for: “Sole” repeatedly emphasises that Scrooge is Marley’s only connection; legal terms (“executor”, “administrator”, “legatee”) make the partnership transactional; the juxtaposition of “sole friend” and “sole mourner” suggests loneliness and possible criticism of a life defined by money.

C1 · 16 marks

Marking: Use a best-fit holistic scale: 1–4 simple relevant comments; 5–8 some supported explanations; 9–12 clear, connected argument with language analysis; 13–16 sustained, discerning analysis across the extract and relevant alternative interpretations.

Illustrative answer / look for: A strong response shows how the repeated assurances of Marley’s death create a strange, comic certainty; how the narrator’s direct address makes death conversational yet ominous; how “sole” in the list of roles implies isolation; and how Scrooge’s “excellent man of business” behaviour at a funeral exposes a possible moral criticism of money taking priority over grief. Alternative readings may note ironic praise, emotional restraint rather than simple cruelty, and the narrator’s playful unreliability. Credit well-evidenced interpretations, not prior knowledge of the rest of the novella.

Recording and feedback

Record A1 /2, A2 /4, A3 /6, B1 /6, B2 /6, C1 /16. Total /40. Identify one specific analytical strength and one next-step target. For example: “Explain how the word choice influences the reader, rather than naming the technique alone.”

Source: Charles Dickens, A Christmas Carol (1843), Stave One; Project Gutenberg eBook #46. Assessment source excerpt punctuation follows that edition. This is an independent NOVA marking scheme, not an exam-board mark scheme.
