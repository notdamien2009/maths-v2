Assessment overview

Unseen prose: The Compass and the Harbour (original fictional passage); six questions, 40 marks, indicative 60 minutes. Focus: comprehension, close analysis of language, symbolism, narrative change, belonging and independence.

Indicative interpretive answer

Leila’s opportunity promises growth yet unsettles her attachment to family and place. The opening market details and the grandmother’s description of a city that “spoke in a hundred small voices” make home an audible, intimate environment. The admission that she wished the boat would “turn around” complicates her ambition. The compass is linked to family memory, but its “hesitated” needle and Sami’s rhetorical question challenge the belief that home is a fixed location. The two imagined futures are presented in parallel: the loss of familiar routines contrasts with exciting questions still to answer. Sami’s “And so will you” reframes change as inevitable rather than as betrayal. At the end, Leila does not consult the compass. Her decision is active but not painless; the market’s voices “travelled with her”, suggesting she can carry belonging into independence. Also accept a more ambivalent reading: the hopeful sunlight is brief, and the journey’s outcome remains unknown.

Marking, reporting and pass rule

Score each criterion independently, total /40. Report comprehension A /8, close analysis B /18, extended interpretation C /14. The usual 70% threshold is 28/40. A pass does not remove the need for feedback on weaknesses.

Suggested feedback: one successful quotation-plus-inference, one precise next step for explanation/analysis, and an optional improvement task (rewrite one paragraph with a better-selected phrase and clearer explanation).
