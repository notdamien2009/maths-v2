The Glasshouse Door · 40 marks · 7 questions.

A1 — 2 marks

Indicative answer: Any TWO of: clock reads 5:55, five minutes until the opening, visitor programmes being arranged, visitors already gathered beyond the gate, promised seedling display prepared for opening.

Mark allocation: 1 mark for each DISTINCT accurate relevant detail, to 2. No credit for two paraphrases of the same detail.

  • Precise reference to lines 1–4
  • Two different preparations or indications of a scheduled opening

A2 — 3 marks

Indicative answer: Mr Fen appears worried and urgently protective: he enters wet and out of breath, tells others to step away, then explains the roof vent is insecure. His later admission of an unreported crack may suggest guilt.

Mark allocation: 1 for a plausible inference about feelings, 1 for precise source evidence from lines 18–23, 1 for explaining how the evidence supports the inference.

  • Plausible feeling
  • Specific evidence
  • Clear connection between evidence and inference

A3 — 4 marks

Indicative answer: For example, the three drops onto the workbench show the roof is leaking (lines 5–6), while the thin dark line creeping beneath the inner door (line 9) suggests worsening water damage. The low metallic tapping beyond the locked door (line 14) raises the possibility of unseen danger.

Mark allocation: For each of TWO different stage directions: 1 mark for accurate identifiable evidence and 1 mark for an explained audience effect (2 × 2).

  • Two distinct stage directions from lines 5–17
  • Audience effect explained for each

A4 — 5 marks

Indicative answer: Initially Maya prioritises a calm successful opening, minimising the rain (“It is only a little rain”). Later she recognises their duty to explain the hazard honestly and chooses to tell visitors with Mr Fen (“We tell them together”). Her shift from reputation to joint responsibility changes the audience’s respect for her.

Mark allocation: 1 early attitude, 1 supporting early detail, 1 later attitude, 1 supporting later detail, 1 explanation of significance/change.

  • Contrasts opening and ending of her attitude
  • Accurate evidence at both points
  • Explains responsibility and consequence

A5 — 6 marks

Indicative answer: Mr Fen admits checking a hinge and failing to report a crack; he now prioritises keeping people clear and calling maintenance, showing guilt and belated accountability. Jonah challenges the false reassurance (“It held yesterday”) and presses to delay rather than risk visitors. Their contrast suggests responsibility means acting on a hazard, not protecting the schedule.

Mark allocation: 1 Mr Fen response, 1 evidence, 1 Jonah response, 1 evidence, 1 meaningful comparison, 1 effect on audience perception of responsibility.

  • Precise evidence for both characters
  • Clear difference in priorities
  • Audience interpretation of responsibility

B1 — 12 marks

Indicative answer: A developed argument might trace storm and roof warnings, the dark line and metallic tap, delays from the knock and clock, conflicting priorities in dialogue, escalated sound, Maya’s interrupted announcement and the blackout. Students may emphasise any coherent selection of evidence showing tension intensifying.

Mark allocation: 0–2 little relevant evidence; 3–5 some accurate observations with limited explanation; 6–8 clear chronological analysis with several techniques and relevant quotation; 9–10 sustained supported interpretation linking language/staging to audience response; 11–12 precise, perceptive and coherently structured analysis of the changing tension and final interruption.

  • Builds across beginning-middle-end, not one moment
  • Explains stage directions/dialogue/pace
  • Uses accurately selected source details
  • Connects techniques to audience response

B2 — 8 marks

Indicative answer: E.g., at lines 36–39 Maya briefly blocks Mr Fen then lowers her hand to show conflict changing into cooperation, pausing before “We tell them together”; at 39–40 let the wind sound grow as her speech breaks and blackout occurs, leaving the audience worried. Use safe theatrical simulation; no real broken glass or danger.

Mark allocation: For each of TWO distinct choices: 1 accurate line(s), 1 practicable direction for voice/movement/sound/light, 2 explanation of how choice communicates meaning to the audience (4 × 2).

  • Two concrete performable choices
  • Specific line references
  • Explicit audience impact for each choice
  • Safe staging
