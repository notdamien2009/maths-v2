Scale factors and maps

Use scale factors across drawings, maps and similar figures.

Learn · five connected concepts

Original explanatory text, no substituted native visual components.

1 · Understanding scale

A scale of 1:25,000 compares a length on a map with the corresponding length on the ground, in the same units.

Demonstrate: 1 cm on the map = 25,000 cm = 250 m in reality.

Common error: Saying 25,000 m instead of 25,000 cm changes the meaning of the ratio.

2 · Consistent units

Convert quantities into matching units before using a ratio. A labelled scale bar offers a useful estimate.

Demonstrate: 3.2 cm at 1:50,000 gives 160,000 cm = 1.6 km.

Common error: Multiplying by 50,000 produces centimetres when the map distance is in centimetres.

3 · Forward and inverse

Map → real: multiply by the denominator. Real → map: divide by the denominator after converting to the same length unit.

Demonstrate: 1.2 km = 120,000 cm; at 1:20,000 the map distance is 6 cm.

Common error: Dividing by 20,000 before converting km to cm is a units error.

4 · Similarity and area

For similar shapes, corresponding lengths use a constant scale factor k. Areas use k², because both dimensions change.

Demonstrate: Length 4 → 10 gives k=2.5; a 6 cm² area enlarges to 6 × 2.5² = 37.5 cm².

Common error: Do not use k for area or add a fixed number to every side.

5 · Transfer and verification

Identify matching dimensions, label units and check with an inverse calculation or estimate. A consistent scale must work for both dimensions.

Demonstrate: Plan 4.8 cm × 3.2 cm at 1:500 means 24 m × 16 m; area 384 m².

Common error: Check 24 m = 2,400 cm and 2,400 ÷ 500 = 4.8 cm.

Examples · worked and guided

1 · Map to real distance

Problem: 1:50,000 and 2.6 cm

Worked solution: 2.6 × 50,000 = 130,000 cm = 1,300 m = 1.3 km.

Guided follow-up

  1. What unit follows 2.6 × 50,000?
  2. How many centimetres are in a kilometre?
  3. Estimate: is 1.3 km plausible?

2 · Real distance to map

Problem: 1:20,000 and 3 km

Worked solution: 3 km = 300,000 cm; 300,000 ÷ 20,000 = 15 cm.

Guided follow-up

  1. Which direction uses division?
  2. Why convert 3 km first?
  3. How does 15×20,000 verify the answer?

3 · Drawing length

Problem: 1:200 and a 7 m room

Worked solution: 7 m = 700 cm; 700 ÷ 200 = 3.5 cm.

Guided follow-up

  1. Identify corresponding lengths.
  2. Why do we divide?
  3. Would 350 cm on paper make sense?

4 · Similar figures

Problem: 6 cm → 15 cm; another side 8 cm

Worked solution: k=15÷6=2.5. The other side becomes 8×2.5=20 cm.

Guided follow-up

  1. Calculate k.
  2. What stays constant across corresponding sides?
  3. Why is adding 9 cm to each side wrong?

5 · Scale drawing and area

Problem: 5 cm × 3 cm at 1:400

Worked solution: Real dimensions are 20 m × 12 m; area 240 m².

Guided follow-up

  1. Convert each dimension to metres.
  2. Multiply real lengths for area.
  3. Why does area scale by the square of the length factor?
